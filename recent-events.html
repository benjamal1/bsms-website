Dr. Hulstyn — "Becoming a Team Physician: The Path to Orthopedic Medicine"

April 30, Salomon 203

About the Speaker

A sports medicine orthopedic surgeon affiliated with Brown since 1993, Dr. Hulstyn serves as a team physician for Brown varsity athletes, has helped care for the Providence Bruins, and teaches medical students, residents, and fellows while maintaining an active surgical practice and ACL research portfolio.

His Path

Born in Amsterdam and raised in the U.S., he started at UCLA before graduating from UC Davis — where he nearly pursued veterinary medicine — then came to Brown for medical school. After early surgical training in California, he returned to Brown for orthopedic residency and completed a sports medicine fellowship at Harvard/Mass General, gaining exposure to the Patriots and Bruins before joining Brown athletics in 1993.

Key Takeaways

  • Sports medicine is broad — orthopedic surgery plus primary care sports medicine, with primary care physicians increasingly serving as the accessible front door for athletes.
  • Availability defines the job — injuries happen at inconvenient times; the reward is longitudinal care, from injury through return to play.
  • History and exam first — listen carefully, let patients tell their story, and use MRI mainly to confirm a well-formed clinical impression.
  • Trust comes from time and follow-through — honest expectation-setting before surgery, and re-evaluating the diagnosis (not blaming the patient) when recovery stalls.
  • Stay close to evidence — in clinic, don't be an outlier without data; in research, question dogma. Be skeptical of every "new and improved" device until the science supports it.
  • AI and robotics: promising, not yet transformative — high surgical volume still predicts the best outcomes; human communication and empathy remain irreplaceable.
  • Prevention is the future — concussion and tackling rules, ACL landing-mechanics programs, and NIL-related liability will reshape the field.

Advice for Students

  • Do research early — but finish projects; a few completed ones beat many unfinished.
  • Beyond the CV, programs weigh work ethic, personality, and being known by residents and faculty.
  • Get involved through residents, labs, interest groups, and clinics — mentorship shaped every stage of his career.
